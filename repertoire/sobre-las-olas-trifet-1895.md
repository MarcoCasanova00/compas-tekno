# Sobre las olas in the Trifet print

Juventino Rosas's waltz appears in the 9 October 1895 issue of the *Boston
Weekly Journal of Sheet Music*, no. 24, F. Trifet & Co., plate 1030.
The date is on the cover. Ball State's Hague Collection holds the issue as
SM-2-196 and marks it No Copyright – United States. The transcription uses
the notation on the first music page, digitized as Page-3. The build includes
new note data and leaves the archival images in their source collection.
[Ball State item record](https://dmr.bsu.edu/digital/collection/ShtMus/id/248/)
· [Page-3 image](https://dmr.bsu.edu/digital/iiif/ShtMus/242/full/full/0/default.jpg)

## The playable excerpt

Two eight-bar phrases start at the printed **1**, after the *Larghetto*
and *Tempo di Valse* introduction. Both staves are in 3/4. The melody starts
B3, then A♯3–B3–D4. It keeps the G4 and F♯4 ties of the first phrase and the
F♯4 and B3 ties of the answer. The second phrase returns to the tonic and
ends with two written rests in the right hand. The left hand keeps the
bass and two chord attacks in every bar. Rehearsal tempo is quarter = 132.
The print says *pp legato*. The excerpt plays at fixed velocity and ends
before the written return of the first theme. No percussion is added.

## First strain through Fine

The return begins like the first theme, then settles on E over A minor in
bars 23–24. The fortissimo octave rise starts at bar 25. Both numbered
endings share the same pitches and rhythms. The second adds Fine and a
fermata at the close. Compás returns to the printed **1** at the
repeat, a reading choice for this excerpt, and plays 64 bars in all. The
fermata has its nominal duration. The two written dynamic levels have
fixed rehearsal equivalents, applied to both hands at the same time.

## Border repertoire and other editions

The Quinteto Típico Mexicano recorded the waltz on Columbia 2553-X, matrix
95432. The Frontera record identifies strings, guitar and mandolin. Arhoolie
reissued the performance as track 102 in its border string-band collection.
The Ibero-Amerikanisches Institut catalog identifies this side as a 1926
New York recording and separately identifies the group's *La Paloma* as
recorded in San Antonio in 1928. The two session locations apply to different
sides. [Frontera disc](https://frontera.library.ucla.edu/recordings/sobre-las-olas-2)
· [Arhoolie reissue](https://folkways.si.edu/orquestas-de-cuerdas-the-string-bands-the-end-of-a-tradition-1926-1938/latin-world/music/album/smithsonian)
· [Egon Ludwig collection catalog](https://www.iai.spk-berlin.de/fileadmin/2_Dokumentenbibliothek/Sammlungen/Sondersammlungen/Sammlung_Egon_Ludwig_Katalog.pdf)

A Thos. Goggan & Bro. Galveston score survives in the Mary D. Hudgins
collection. The IAML presentation reproduces a cover and interior spread
from a Goggan edition. Berkeley also catalogs a separate San Francisco
piano edition. These are routes for comparing print circulation. The
playable excerpt is based on the dated Trifet print.
[Galveston holding](https://uark.as.atlas-sys.com/repositories/2/archival_objects/798152)
· [IAML cover and spread](https://www.iaml.info/sites/default/files/pdf/piscitelli_texas_rome_2016.pdf)
· [Berkeley holding](https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/record/99518)

The U.S. public-domain assessment applies to the 1895 notation and its
transcription. The recordings, later arrangements and other territories
are tracked separately. The introduction, the following strain and its D.C. route, and
Waltz No. 2 remain in the source edition.
