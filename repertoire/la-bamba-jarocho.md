# La Bamba · Veracruz accompaniment

Choose **La Bamba · Veracruz accompaniment** in the Compás rehearsal
hub. At 136 BPM the two 2/4 bars put the tonic and subdominant on the
first two quarter notes, then keep the dominant for two beats.
The initial slot gives a dry strum cue. Use PRESETS during playback
to add the bass, then the optional footwork cue. A live jarana player
can supply the chords and a singer or requinto player can carry
the melody. The Band controls keep the same live part cues.

| Slot | Audible parts |
| --- | --- |
| Bamba Strum | Strum cue alone |
| Bamba with Bass | Strum plus roots through I–IV–V–V |
| Bamba Footwork | Strum, bass and a dry footwork cue |

The cycle comes from Carol A. Hess’s guide to José Gutiérrez and
Los Hermanos Ochoa, Smithsonian Folkways SFW40505_101. The guide
counts twelve cycles before the vocal at about 0:21 and follows
the verses and instrumental interlude. Its timing puts four quarter
notes per cycle at roughly 137 BPM, so 136 is an editorial rehearsal
tempo. Don Stigberg’s Veracruz interview describes the song as
a fast duple exception among many compound-meter sones. Folkways
credits Felipe Ochoa on harp, Marcos Ochoa on jarana and José
Gutiérrez Ramos on requinto. The recording and interview support
the meter, harmonic cycle and playing roles. Compás supplies the
strum placement, register, pickups, footwork and fill. The footwork
is a synthetic time cue. The three slots start with the 727 kit off.
[Hess guide, PDF p. 22](https://content.ucpress.edu/title/9780520285583/hess_ch6oag.pdf#page=22) ·
[Folkways track](https://folkways.si.edu/jose-gutierrez-los-hermanos-ochoa/la-bamba/latin-world/music/track/smithsonian) ·
[Universidad Veracruzana interview](https://www.uv.mx/gaceta/Gaceta67/67/ventana/ventana03.htm)

The first Compás bass note in each beat uses semitone offsets
0, 5, 7 and 7. In the default key that is C, F, G and G.
The original strum has downbeat emphasis and quicker anticipations.
Lift adds an extra late stroke in each bar. Break keeps the strum
and leaves the melody and harmony to the musician. The arrangement
contains no recorded performance, lyric text, lead line or score
data from an archived edition.

## Keep the witnesses separate

- Indiana University dates the available Álvaro Hernández Ortiz
  performance, billed as El Jarocho, to 1939. That date describes the
  archive’s identified performance. It leaves earlier sources open.
  [Cook Music Library](https://collections.libraries.indiana.edu/cookmusiclibrary/exhibits/show/sounds_of_mexico/la_bamba)
- Ritchie Valens’s 1958 Del-Fi side, the Los Lobos 1987 film track,
  and Selena’s commercial release are distinct arrangements.
  The surviving Selena clip is attributed to Canales’s Matamoros
  show in 1987. The exact taping date and playback source remain open.
  [Valens registry](https://www.loc.gov/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/recording-registry/registry-by-induction-years/2018/) ·
  [AFI film record](https://catalog.afi.com/Catalog/MovieDetails/57682) ·
  [Selena commercial issue](https://music.apple.com/us/song/1442962335) ·
  [Broadcast clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_6kFS__GBrE) ·
  [Matamoros reporting](https://heraldodemexico.com.mx/tendencias/2021/3/5/asi-fueron-los-conciertos-de-selena-en-tamaulipas-en-uno-canto-la-bamba-videos-266168-amp.html)
- UNT catalogs Luis Berber’s piano score in the George Bragg
  collection, Box 2, item 18. SGAE Madrid holds Ángel Currás’s
  manuscript AS-4557. The archive descriptions identify those
  arrangements. The actual notation and its reuse basis remain open.
  [UNT finding aid](https://archives.library.unt.edu/repositories/3/archival_objects/284377) ·
  [SGAE manuscript record](https://archivo.sgae.es/heritageobject/la-bamba/)
