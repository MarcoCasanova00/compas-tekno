# Lamento Borincano · score intake

Rafael Hernández Marín's bolero reached record buyers through New York's
Puerto Rican music scene. Canario y Su Grupo recorded Victor 30008 on
14 July 1930. The surviving take is BVE-62790-2, with Pedro Ortiz Dávila
(Davilita) singing. Mario Cancel-Bigay describes guitars, maracas and
claves in this performance and reports that it leaves out one of the
song's stanzas. The recording provides an early ensemble reference.
For a Compás version based on the sheet music, preserve the edition's
form and keep any later percussion backing separately credited.

The ERIC teaching volume ED059933 reproduces the Brito score with
permission from Peer. PDF pages 33–34 are printed pages 23–24. SHA-256
of the retained PDF is
`e5058438ec9c8a610487c9a01ffb1d0ffa3e95e489d340a25eeda79a48ccefe9`.
The date and renewal in the catalogs distinguish Brito's setting from
the earlier deposit. Applying the Copyright Office's 95-year term to
EP30072 puts the end of the U.S. term at the end of 2027. The recording
has its own term. The Set Builder links the scan as a reference. The
notation in EU25702 still needs inspection before deciding which
passages can be sourced from Hernández's earlier version.

| Where to look | What the Brito print shows |
| --- | --- |
| Printed p. 23, systems 1–2 | Andantino in cut time, four flats, piano introduction. The right hand introduces the pickup figures over held piano tones. |
| Printed p. 23, systems 3–4 | Segno at the first vocal entrance. F-minor opening, then C7 and D-flat. First and second ending brackets take the two text passes through different exits. |
| Printed p. 24, system 1 and start of system 2 | Continuation in the four-flat signature. The harmony goes through E-flat and D-flat before the C7 passage. |
| Printed p. 24, system 2 through the last left system | The signature changes to one flat before the F-major vocal section. The first ending carries a pickup into the next pass. The second ending carries the D.S. instruction and coda sign. |
| Printed p. 24, last right system | Detached coda marked *Menos*. The printed B-flat minor moves to F, then the final held cadence and *Fin*. |

Keep the segno, both ending pairs and detached coda as separate entries
in the transcription ledger. When the edition can be used for playback,
confirm the full route after D.S. before assigning live cue targets,
including whether the repeats are taken again. The marked *Menos* needs
its own tempo decision. Cue names in Compás would identify the piano
introduction, vocal entrance, major section and coda.

McGill’s Marvin Duchow Music Library lists a voice-and-piano score
under CA MDML 015-2-1143, with Alfredo Brito as arranger. Madrid’s
Inv 12028(11) is also a Brito setting, issued in Barcelona. The
Biblioteca Nacional de Puerto Rico lists the song in Peer’s *Song
Folio*, call number PR 780.97295 H557s, without identifying the setting.
These are additional holdings to compare with the ERIC print.

The actionable source request is the **deposited notation** for
`EU25702`, dated **31 July 1930**, by Rafael Hernández of New York.
The Library of Congress Music Division can advise on which deposit or
copy survives. Ask for the score associated with that number and its
cover, stamps and complete music pages to verify the edition.
