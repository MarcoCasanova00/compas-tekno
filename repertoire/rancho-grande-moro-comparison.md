# Allá en el Rancho Grande · Moro, Lomax and Jiménez

The UNT scan reproduces the Texas Folklore Society’s *Southwestern Lore*,
vol. IX, in a 1965 facsimile with the Society’s 1931 copyright notice.
The chapter opening on p. 118 credits **Joaquin Moro**, although the table
of contents and UNT metadata use **Mora**. The song occupies p. 119
alone, scan sequence 125. There is no composer line on that page.

## What the two melody prints show

Both use one sharp, an eighth-note pickup and a 2/4 signature. Count
the next bar as measure 1. The melody is complete across 20 measures.
The verse begins at measure 9 and the page ends with a final double
rule. There are no repeat dots or piano staves. The Lomax print names
Silvano R. Ramos, references the Texas Folk-Lore Society and marks
the tempo moderately slow. The corresponding pitch sequence agrees
across the two witnesses, including the sharpened lead-in to D near
the middle of the first line. Two note values differ.

| Measure | Moro, p. 119 | Lomax, 1934 exhibit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6, near the end of the first system | Three eighth notes occupy the whole written bar. | The middle note is a quarter, completing 2/4. |
| 8, just before the double bar | An eighth and a quarter. The last stem meets the staff lines but the scan shows neither an augmentation dot nor a rest. | An eighth and a dotted quarter, completing 2/4. |
| 9 | The verse enters immediately after the double bar. | The same verse begins partway across the third system. |

A literal 24-PPQ read gives the two short measures 36 ticks each and
the complete melody 948 ticks, including the 12-tick pickup. Reading
the later Lomax lengths gives 972 ticks. A regular dance accompaniment
would need an explicit choice between these timings. The Marks
voice-and-piano print, plate 10,129-3, supplies its own four-bar
introduction and D.S. to Fine route.

## San Antonio conjunto witness

Santiago Jiménez y sus Valedores recorded the tune for Imperial
236-A, matrix DI-285. Arhoolie’s track 17 notes place the recording in
San Antonio around 1948. They credit Jiménez on accordion and vocal
and give Lorenzo Caballero on guitar and Ismael González on bass
with a qualification. Arhoolie lists the title as a ranchera and
describes Jiménez’s performance as a polka version. Frontera’s 78
entry classifies it as a corrido and tags it instrumental. Keep
those catalog descriptions separate. The liner notes give the
stronger lead for date and personnel. This recording is the
conjunto listening reference for bass and guitar placement. The
Moro print supplies only the melody and its written timing.
[Imperial 78](https://frontera.library.ucla.edu/recordings/el-rancho-grande-24) ·
[Arhoolie booklet, printed p. 23](https://folkways-media.si.edu/docs/folkways/artwork/ARH07001.pdf#page=12)

## Reuse review

No matching volume or chapter title was found in the Stanford book
renewal dataset or the NYPL transcriptions of 1958, 1959 and 1960
Part 1 renewals. Searches included the volume title, chapter title,
both Moro and Mora spellings and Texas Folklore Society. The 1931
book catalog OCR supplied no matching original registration under
those titles or publisher variants. The search did not establish
the status of a separately registered contribution or musical
edition. The 1965 facsimile and UNT scan also have their own
provenance. A 1931 U.S. term, if renewed and measured from that
publication, would run through 2026. Keep this card available for
reference while the early score or a reuse basis is resolved.
The most useful exact witnesses are the 1927 two-voice deposit
E 663020 and Morgan 159383.
