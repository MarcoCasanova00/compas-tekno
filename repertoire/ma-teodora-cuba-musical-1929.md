# La Má Teodora in Cuba musical, 1929

The retained page is printed p. 25 of *Cuba musical*, edited by José
Calero Martín and Leopoldo Valdés Quesada, Imprenta de Molina y Compañía,
Havana, 1929. IMSLP file 1061279 identifies the parent FIU record. The
downloaded one-page scan has SHA-256
`5744a7834f76920d914b5d56beb66f25ff9353baaccb2fac80abca3cb22356f4`.
[IMSLP edition record](https://imslp.org/wiki/Son_de_la_m%C3%A1_Teodora_(Gin%C3%A9s,_Teodora))
· [Exact score scan](https://s9.imslp.org/files/imglnks/usimg/6/68/IMSLP1061279-PMLP1647556-gines_la_ma_teodora.pdf)

The page calls this a Cuban popular song and dates it to the seventeenth
century. Alberto Muguercia's comparison of Laureano Fuentes Matons's
1893 account with the sources it invokes documents discrepancies. Karoline
Bahrs studies the Dominican perspectives on the song and its origin
story. This MIDI documents the 1929 notation. The original authorship
and date of the song remain unresolved.
[Muguercia, 1971](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/AA/00/06/82/07/00068/Revista%20BNJM_1971_septiembre-diciembre.pdf)
· [Bahrs, 2011](https://www.jstor.org/stable/41348253)

The 6/8 bar has 72 ticks at 24 PPQ. Each bracketed three-quarter figure
occupies one bar, with three equal attacks across the two dotted beats.
The chordal responses are preserved on one pitched MIDI track. The MIDI
uses the nominal written quarter for the fermata and a rehearsal tempo of
72 dotted quarters per minute. Velocity values 62, 46 and 32 represent
the printed p, pp and ppp. The four echoes are written out. The last dyad
releases 48 ticks before the end marker, leaving the final rests intact.
